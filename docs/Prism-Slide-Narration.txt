SLIDE 1

Hello everyone. My Week One project is Prism, a local web application that helps marketing analysts check claims about campaign performance.

The problem is that an overall conversion rate can improve even when individual customer groups perform worse. If a team relies only on that average, it may increase its budget without understanding what changed.

Prism compares the overall result with each customer group, shows changes in audience mix, and explains whether a selected claim is supported by the data.


SLIDE 2

Here is the story behind the demo. A marketing manager sees conversion rise from 20 percent to 45.6 percent and considers increasing the budget.

But in our synthetic dataset, new customers fall from 10 to 8 percent, and returning customers fall from 60 to 55 percent. Both groups decline.

The audience has shifted toward returning customers, who already convert more often. So the overall increase is real, but it does not mean every group improved or that the campaign caused the change. Prism makes that distinction visible.


SLIDE 3

Prism runs entirely in the browser. A CSV or sample dataset enters validation, then the app calculates overall rates, group rates, and a comparison that holds the audience mix constant.

Explicit rules check the selected claim, and the interface shows the evidence. Sandbox edits reuse those calculations. The evaluation page checks the rules against twelve fixed reference cases.

I used Codex to help build and refine the application. It needs no paid APIs, and its answers come from calculations rather than a live AI model.
